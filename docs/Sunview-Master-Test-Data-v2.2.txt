4.3 Why one account per household
Version 2.1 gave each named purchaser their own login. On SV-1001 that produced two different signatures on two change
orders — CO-1002 by Liam, CO-1004 by Sarah — with nothing in the record establishing whether either signature bound the
other purchaser. On a four-purchaser contract like the Patel household, the same arrangement would make it unclear which
of four people could authorise a change order, and whether the other three had agreed.
The model is therefore: the household holds one portal account; the contract names every purchaser; one purchaser is
designated authorized signatory and is the only party who can approve or e-sign; the signature record captures the named
individual, the account, and the authority basis.
Concern 	Rule
Who can log in 	One shared household account per project.
Who can approve a change order 	The designated authorized signatory only.
What the signature record stores 	Named individual · household account · authority basis · timestamp · IP.
What co-purchasers receive 	All contractual notices by email, and read access is available if a second login is later granted.
Above a value threshold 	All named purchasers countersign, rather than the signatory alone — threshold to be set,
see 24.13.
Changing the signatory 	Requires PM action and writes an audit entry. Not self-service.
A shared household login is a deliberate trade-off. It removes approval ambiguity but weakens attribution, because the account
alone no longer identifies who acted. That is why the signature record must name the individual and the authority basis, not
merely the account.
